# Hi, I'm Udo 

I'm a computational biologist and data scientist working at the intersection of **bioinformatics, statistics, software engineering, and infectious disease research**.

I originally trained in bioinformatics and machine learning, then spent several years working as a **Data Scientist at Booking.com and SoundCloud**. Most of that work lived behind company walls, so there is not much of it to see here. After returning to academic research, I decided I wanted to do things a little differently and make more of the software I build publicly available whenever possible.

## 🧬 Pathogen surveillance in Freiburg

I am currently working at the **University Clinic Freiburg**, where my focus has shifted towards reproducible bioinformatics software for genomic pathogen surveillance.

Some of the things I am currently building include:

- **[NUM-SAR Norovirus pipeline](https://github.com/the-foxlab/NoroTracer)**, a Nextflow workflow for analysing nationwide norovirus amplicon sequencing data, with the longer-term goal of supporting outbreak analysis and identification of transmission links.
- **[NGS variant detection pipeline](https://github.com/the-foxlab/CVG_Amplicon_Flow)**, a Snakemake-based workflow used internally for reproducible processing and variant calling of viral sequencing data.

Much of my current work revolves around **Python, Nextflow, Snakemake, containers, NGS data, statistics, and reproducible computational workflows**.

## 🦇 Ancient RNA viruses

During my PhD at Charité Berlin, I worked on recovering and analysing RNA virus genomes from historical museum specimens. 
I was able to revover a **Bandavirus genome from a bat collected in 1919**. This was one of the oldest sequenced mammalian RNA viruses at the time and provides a unique opportunity to study long term evolutionary processes in RNA arboviruses. The work was published in the journal [Virus Evolution](https://doi.org/10.1093/ve/veag034).

The project combined NGS data processing, phylogenetics, evolutionary analysis, and experimental virology.

[Ancient RNA virus project](https://github.com/UdoGi/ancient_bunyavirus)

During this time at the [Institute of Virology](https://github.com/VirologyCharite) I was very fortunate to have had supervision of this work by **Christian Drosten and [Terry Jones](https://github.com/terrycojones)**, and owe both of them a big thank you for the guidance, discussions, and freedom to explore some fairly unusual ideas involving century-old bats and RNA viruses.
